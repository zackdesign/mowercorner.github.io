A chainsaw is the machine people most often bring in convinced it's finished,
when what it actually needs is a sharp chain, fresh fuel and a service. Blunt
chains make the saw work harder, burn more fuel, and wear the bar and sprocket
out early.

## Chain sharpening

Bring the chain on its own, or leave the saw with us and pick it up ready to
cut. If the chain is stretched past adjustment, or the drive links are damaged,
we'll say so rather than sharpen something that's going to let you down halfway
through a load of firewood. We carry replacement chains and bars in a range of
sizes and pitches.

**A chain is due for sharpening when** the saw produces fine dust instead of
coarse chips, you have to lean on it to make it cut, or it pulls to one side
through the cut.

## Chainsaw servicing and repairs

Our workshop handles the full job, not just the sharp end:

- Carburettor clean, tune and rebuild
- Air filters, fuel filters and spark plugs
- Fuel and oil lines, and the oiler that keeps the bar alive
- Starter recoils and pull cords
- Bar, sprocket and chain wear
- Battery saw diagnostics using STIHL testing equipment

We're an **Elite STIHL Dealer**, so STIHL machinery, accessories and spare parts
are on the shelf here — but the workshop services every brand that comes through
the door.

## Bars, chains, oil and safety gear

Beyond the saw itself, we keep the consumables you'll actually run out of:
two-stroke oil, bar and chain oil, files and sharpening kits, replacement bars
and chains. Ask at the counter — if we haven't got it in front of you, we can
usually get it in from our [spare parts range](/spare-parts/).

If you're cutting firewood regularly, it's worth talking about protective gear
too. Chaps, helmets and gloves aren't glamorous, but a chainsaw doesn't care how
experienced you are.

## Before firewood season

The rush hits when the weather turns. A saw that's been in the shed since last
winter almost always needs fresh fuel and a service before it will start
reliably — and the queue in autumn is a lot longer than the one in summer.
